# CyberResearch Radar methodology

CyberResearch Radar is a source-first watch for cybersecurity and cryptography research opportunities. Its scope reflects senior academic practice across applied cryptography, post-quantum cryptography, homomorphic encryption, privacy-preserving machine learning, systems and network security, digital forensics, cybercrime, trustworthy AI, IoT/cloud security and cybersecurity education.

## What is included

- peer-reviewed conferences and journal-conference venues;
- workshops, including calls for papers, talks and workshop proposals;
- book chapters and journal special issues;
- research schools and doctoral or postdoctoral opportunities;
- competitive research grants and consortium calls.

Every public record stores its official page, deadline evidence, verification date, geographic fields, topic tags and a confidence status. Records without a confirmed deadline remain on the watchlist. Closed editions may remain visible so the Monday process can detect the next edition.

## Venue rankings

Rankings are displayed as separate evidence, never combined into a universal score.

- **ICORE/CORE Conference Rankings 2026** is the primary A*, A, B and C reference for computer-science conference series.
- **CCF Recommended International Academic Conferences and Journals** may be shown as a distinct A, B or C signal.
- **Scopus, CiteScore, SJR and SNIP** are source-level indexing and metric signals, principally for journals, book series and some proceedings. They are not converted into CORE or CCF ranks.
- **JCR/JIF** applies to journals and is not a conference ranking.

A workshop, demo track, short-paper track or companion event never inherits the rank of its parent conference. A rank is displayed only when the series match and edition have been checked against the reference source.

## Journal targeting records

The journal directory is designed to support a targeting decision, not to name a universally “best” journal. Each record keeps the following dimensions separate:

- current scientific scope and explicit exclusions from the publisher or journal;
- publisher, scholarly society, ISSN, article types, peer-review model and publication frequency;
- access model, optional or mandatory APC, currency, taxes/conditions, waivers, submission fees and other stated charges;
- publisher-reported first-decision, post-review, acceptance and online-publication timing, when available;
- indexing claims, JCR/JIF, Scopus/CiteScore, SJR quartiles and CCF recommendations, each with its own edition and source;
- data, code, reproducibility and publication-ethics policies;
- a caution status for discontinued, delisted or otherwise high-risk titles.

The cybersecurity, privacy, cryptography and digital-forensics coverage is reconciled against a 2025 SJR sweep, then enriched from official publisher or society pages and identity registries such as ISSN/Crossref. Titles whose publication has ceased are retained in the research audit but are not offered as current submission targets. An active journal can still carry a caution when its indexing has been discontinued or when a look-alike website creates an identity risk.

Publisher timeline statistics can include desk rejections and are not promises for an individual manuscript. The interface labels a response value as a reported median, an editorial target or a scheduled review cycle so these signals are not presented as equivalent. Prices can change by submission date, article type, tax status and institutional agreement. Missing values are shown as “not published”; they are never estimated.

The journal CCF signals use the seventh edition of the CCF recommended international conference and journal directory, formally released on 31 March 2026. CCF ranks remain separate from JCR and SJR quartiles.

## Monday refresh contract

The scheduled watcher runs every Monday at 06:00 UTC.

1. It reopens the official evidence page for each deadline.
2. It refreshes a deadline's verification date only when the date appears in submission context; exact time and AoE/UTC confirmation are tracked separately in the report.
3. It closes past calls but retains the record for future-edition detection.
4. It scans a curated registry of official society, publisher and funding pages for new candidate links.
5. New discoveries enter a review queue; they do not receive an inferred rank or an invented deadline.
6. A previously reviewed record is not silently downgraded after one parser miss or bot block; it is flagged in the machine-readable review count.
7. The watch report records source health, content fingerprints and candidate dates, and the job alerts when most discovery registries fail.
8. Journal evidence pages are fingerprinted separately; price, timing, metric or policy changes enter a review queue instead of being silently copied into public records.

The runtime is deterministic and requires no private API key. This keeps the public refresh auditable and inexpensive. Human review remains the approval gate for new public records, ranking changes and ambiguous deadline updates.

## Editorial safeguards

- Official organisers, societies, publishers and funders are preferred over aggregators.
- A deadline timezone is preserved when the organiser gives one; AoE means UTC-12.
- Prospective indexation claims are labelled as such and never presented as confirmed indexing.
- Multi-cycle venues show the nearest open deadline and retain later cycles in notes.
- Preliminary, tentative and timezone-unspecified calls are flagged for weekly rechecking.
- The portal does not assess an individual researcher or article through a journal or venue metric.

## Community corrections

Researchers can submit a new call or correction through the repository issue form. A useful report contains an official CFP URL, exact deadline and timezone, event dates, location, opportunity type and any ranking evidence.
